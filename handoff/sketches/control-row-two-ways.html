The control row, two ways

The same three choices — year, arrangement, colour — over the same map. Option 1 keeps them as controls and makes all three the same kind; option 2 folds them into the title so the row reads as one sentence about what the map shows. Both are live: press the buttons, pick from the blanks.

1Three of the same button

The year stops being a dropdown — the only control in the row of a different species — and becomes a third segmented pair like View and Colour. Labels sit in the muted ink at 12.5px so the buttons carry; the year pair needs no label, the numbers are the label. One height, one border, one active state.

2The title is the sentence

No button row at all. The title says what the map is — All industries, shown as a treemap, coloured by sector, in 2024 — and the three words that could be otherwise are the controls: teal, bold, underlined, with a chevron. Nothing here is a box competing with the map's colours; the only ink is the title's own.

Which one

Option 1 is the honest control row: three choices, three identical switches, the reader sees every alternative at once and never has to open anything. Its cost is that it is a row of six teal boxes above a field of nine colours — orderly, but it is still furniture.

Option 2 puts the furniture away. The reader reads a sentence, notices three words are different, and the whole row weighs no more than a title. Its cost is that the alternatives are hidden until a blank is opened, so "Ranked" and "Complexity" are discovered rather than seen — and a reader who never opens a blank may never learn the map has other forms.

If the map is the point and the controls are a courtesy, take 2. If the two arrangements and two colourings are things you want every reader to try, take 1.
