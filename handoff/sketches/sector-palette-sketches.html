Nine sectors, nine colours

The treemap keeps its nine sector colours. Today three of them are the same warm red — professional, education & health and trade & transportation, which happen to be the three largest blocks and sit side by side — and for colour-blind readers manufacturing, construction and other collapse into one. Each sketch below tiles the real 2024 map (877 industries) in a candidate palette, shows it as a deuteranope and a protanope would see it, and scores the closest pair of colours (OKLab ΔE ×100; 8 is the target, under 5 is a merge). The strip under each key sets the swatches beside the page's own teal, tier chips and complexity ramp.

  • Vary lightness, not just hue. Tableau 10 and Observable 10 separate confusable colours by lightness; Datawrapper's guide says the same, and that a palette should still read in greyscale.
  • Off the pure hues, softer on the big areas. Datawrapper: shift 5–10° off the primaries; desaturate light colours, put the saturation in the dark ones. Large blocks in loud colour tire the eye.
  • Nine is the ceiling. Paul Tol's muted scheme is the only published 9-colour set rated colour-blind safe; ColorBrewer's safe palettes stop at four. Past eight, position and labels have to carry some of the load — the map has both.
  • Keep the encodings apart. Teal already means the brand, the traded tier and the complex end of the ramp, so no sector should sit on it.
