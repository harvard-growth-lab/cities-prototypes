Opt-2 of the key study, thought again: five places the two verbs can live so that nothing stands over the map — and how much the key says about a sector while it offers them. Every frame is live over the 2024 rows: point at the key, then hide a sector or keep only it. "Show all" and the crumbs bring everything back.

            Where each one stands

            Nothing over the map: all five. A and E drop below the key, over the foot; B, C and D never leave the key's band.

            What they say about the sector: B and D say only its name (the colour and the entry already do); A, C and E carry one line of figures — jobs, share of the metro, how many industries.

            Reachable by a keyboard and a thumb: B (the verbs are in the tab order behind the entry), C (the buttons stay put), E (a menu). A and D ask the pointer to travel to them.

            My reading: C first, then A. C gives the figures a fixed home and keeps every verb in one place, and nothing on the row moves; A is the card the reader knows, turned to drop below the key. B is the quietest but reflows the row under the pointer; D moves with the entry; E costs a click.
