Nine More Trees

The constraint section has to do two jobs at once as it narrows: keep the whole tree (up to four branches) on screen as a map of where the reader is in the framework, while giving the fork logic — the pizza chart, the per-branch instruments, the rail copy — enough room to actually be read. The three shipped small-stage modes (fit / focus / detail) all answer the same question: what does the camera do inside a fixed, vertical, single-purpose stage? Each family below breaks one word of that question — the optics (04–06), the vertical (07–08), the single-purpose (09–11), and finally the stage itself (12).

Baseline

The three already on the switch

Numbered 01–03 here so the new options can keep counting. All three live as entries on the FIT_MODES segmented control — which is worth keeping in mind below: that switch is already an experiment harness, and several new options can land as just another entry on it.

Family A · 04–06

More zoom & pan — change the optics

Keep the vertical tree and the stage; make the camera and the rendering smarter. These are the closest relatives of focus and detail, and the cheapest to probe — they mostly fix the 1200–1400px laptop squeeze rather than the phone.

04

Semantic zoom

lands as: a FIT_MODES entry

Follow-the-path already moves a camera; this makes zoom mean something. Each zoom band renders a different drawing of the same nodes: far is the minimap’s dots-and-elbows drawn on the stage itself, mid is one-word chips, near is full cards with copy. Scroll drives the camera as it does now; the representation crossfades at thresholds. Zoomed out, the stage is the map — no separate minimap needed.

Where the fork logic lives
Instruments park at fixed spots beside their fork node, so the camera arrives at each chart — the reading is staged where the question is asked.
How the map survives
The far level is the map; any zoom-out recovers the whole structure on the stage itself.
Reuses
branchBox / fitTransform for the camera; BranchMinimap’s drawing becomes the far LOD.
Sharpest risk
Three renderings of every node to author and keep in sync. Thresholds feel arbitrary unless principled — snap exactly where a level’s type would cross LEGIBLE_PX, which the stage already measures.
05

Fisheye accordion

lands as: a FIT_MODES entry

Detail-on-route is binary: the walked branch gets everything, the others fold to a count. Make it continuous instead — every branch is always drawn as a column, and width redistributes: the focused column takes the room its full-size cards need, the others compress into narrow spines (head chip on top, leaves as ticks). Focus follows the walk, but hover or tap borrows it, so peeking at a sibling branch never costs your place.

Where the fork logic lives
Inside the focused column’s width — the instrument threads between the question card and the leaves, at full size, because the column is sized to fit it.
How the map survives
All branches remain visible, colored, and tappable places — the map and the detail are the same object at two compression levels.
Reuses
detailLeafBoxes already re-spaces the walked leaf row across freed room; this generalizes its width math to whole columns.
Sharpest risk
Compressed spines must look pressable, not decorative; truncated labels need a hover/tap reveal; and the constant re-layout as focus moves has to feel like breathing, not churn.
06

The loupe

lands as: a FIT_MODES entry

Accept the shrink. The whole tree stays fit-whole at any width — and below the legibility floor its labels simply fade out, leaving pure shape: the honest map. A lens rides with the traveller and renders whatever it covers at native size; the lens is the only reading surface. Parked on a question node, it snaps open into a card; dragged anywhere else, it reads that neighborhood instead. Touch-native by accident — a loupe is already a finger-sized object.

Where the fork logic lives
The lens has “park” states: on a fork it grows into the question card; the instrument opens beside the tree as now, or as a full-bleed interlude on narrow stages.
How the map survives
Completely — the base layer is never cropped, folded, or re-laid; it just stops pretending its labels are readable.
Reuses
fit mode as-is for the base; the lens is the same SVG rendered once more under a clip with a bigger transform — cheap.
Sharpest risk
Reading multi-line cards through a porthole is fussy; the park-state must be generous or it’s a gimmick. Screen-reader and keyboard paths must not live inside the lens.

Family B · 07–08

Turn the tree — change the geometry

The deeper problem: the tree’s widest rank (four heads, or the full leaf row) competes for the screen’s scarcest axis. Both options here move the crowded rank onto an axis that has room — the scroll direction, or the ring of a circle the section already owns.

07

The sideways tree

lands as: a shape-level transpose

Root at the left, leaves at the right: depth runs across in three columns, and siblings stack downward, where scroll makes room effectively infinite. Cards become rows — the multi-line labels finally get set horizontally, the way text wants. The traveller walks in reading direction, which suits the story (“a diagnostic pipeline”) as well as descent ever did. On a phone it degrades gracefully into an outline view: heads as rows, the walked one expanding inline, the instrument slotting in as a full-width card between its question and its leaves. The quad shape — the worst case for width — becomes the easiest case: four rows.

Where the fork logic lives
In the blank “future” space right of the walk’s frontier on wide stages; inline between question and leaves in the narrow outline.
How the map survives
The whole tree fits any width by construction — only its height varies, and height is scroll.
Reuses
Everything in walkShapes is authored x/y — this is a transpose of the row constants into column constants; buses, elbows, and the traveller survive rotated.
Sharpest risk
The chart→root handoff beat is choreographed for a top-rooted tree, and the rail copy speaks in descents (“drops to its side”). This changes the section’s signature move, not just its layout — the thing to test is whether the walk still feels like a walk.
08

The chart is the map

lands as: a new shape / telling

The section maintains two figures — the plane and the tree — and parks one to show the other. But branches already are sectors: PlaneSector tiles the full 360°. So draw the framework on the plane: root at the origin, branch heads on a first ring at their sector anchors, leaves on an outer ring — angle chooses the branch, radius is depth. The walk becomes a journey outward from the center, and the city’s dot never has to “carry to the root”: it is already home. A square scales to any width far more gracefully than a 1180-unit-wide tree.

Where the fork logic lives
Fork one is literally visible — the sector boundary the dot sits inside. Fork two opens as a panel beside the square (or below it, narrow), reading the active sector.
How the map survives
There is only one figure, and it is always whole; the tree can never scroll away from the chart because they are the same object.
Reuses
sectorAnchor / sectorPoly and the whole plane model; the chart beats are unchanged — the tree beats stop leaving the chart.
Sharpest risk
Fork two isn’t a planar direction (cost vs. constraint), so radius must be sold as “one question deeper”; ring labels crowd at 4 branches × 2 leaves. The strongest version may be the quad shape — where the quadrant is the diagnosis, this drawing is simply the truth.

Family C · 09–11

Split the jobs — map and reading surface stop sharing

The stage is overloaded: it is asked to be the map of the framework and the surface where each fork’s logic gets read. These three give each job its own real estate — the map becomes chrome, a swappable camera, or a sheet — so neither job has to lose.

09

The tree-shaped stepper

lands as: section chrome

Promote the minimap from fallback to the map. A slim strip — left edge on wide screens, sticky under the header on narrow ones — permanently shows the whole structure with the lit route and the you-dot. The stage is then free to give each beat one full-bleed thing: the question card large, the instrument at hero size, the leaf choice as big tappable cards. It’s a wizard whose stepper is shaped like the actual decision tree instead of a row of dots. Context stops being negotiated per-beat; it’s guaranteed by the frame.

Where the fork logic lives
The entire stage. INSET_HERO stops being an inset — each instrument simply is the scene for its beat.
How the map survives
Constant and structural: the strip is always there, always whole, always lit with the route — the one place it can’t be crowded out of.
Reuses
BranchMinimap is already this component; the beat schedule maps one-to-one onto scenes. The cheapest probe on this page.
Sharpest risk
The tree stops being the theater. The traveller now lives in a 60px strip, and the physical descent — the section’s signature — shrinks with it. Whether the walk still lands emotionally is exactly what the probe should test.
10

Two cameras trade places

lands as: a FIT_MODES entry

Keep both panels mounted at all times: the whole tree, and the detail (the instrument, or the zoomed fork). The beat decides which one is hero and which is the thumbnail — and tapping the thumbnail swaps them at any moment, so the reader can always trade “where am I” for “let me read that” and back. The section already half-does this in one direction (the chart parks into the inset when the tree arrives; the instrument takes the hero panel in the short flow). This makes the relationship symmetric and hands the reader the swap.

Where the fork logic lives
The hero panel, at whatever size the stage affords — it no longer has to fit beside a full tree.
How the map survives
As the thumbnail camera — degraded gracefully, but recoverable instantly and manually, which the current modes never allow.
Reuses
INSET / INSET_HERO geometry and the park animation; both cameras render the same SVG under two transforms, so the swap is one transition.
Sharpest risk
The swap animation must be flawless or it disorients; thumbnail tap targets are tight on phones; and a reader who swaps mid-beat needs the scroll story to still make sense when they swap back.
11

The bottom-sheet map

lands as: the narrow companion

Phone-native job split: the narrative and instruments become the page’s main scroll, and the tree persists as a bottom sheet. Collapsed, it peeks as a one-line schematic — root ▸ your branch ▸ your leaf, with the unwalked branches as dimmed stubs. Pulled up, it’s the whole tree at full width. The sheet position becomes the reader’s own context dial, in a gesture every phone user already owns. Not a desktop mode at all — it pairs with whatever the wide stage does.

Where the fork logic lives
The main scroll — instruments and copy get the full column, uncontested, exactly when the walk reaches them.
How the map survives
One gesture away, plus the always-visible peek strip. Weaker than always-on — but honest about what a phone can hold.
Reuses
The minimap drawing for the peek strip; wholeBox fit for the pulled state; diagnose() untouched.
Sharpest risk
Sheets fight browser edge gestures; and “context on demand” teaches the framework less firmly than context always-on — the peek strip has to carry more weight than it looks like it does.

Family D · 12

Let the page be the tree

The most radical move: on narrow screens, stop fitting the tree to a stage at all. There is no stage — which means nothing to squeeze.

12

The subway spine

lands as: a third telling (flow-level)

The walked route draws as one continuous line down the page; scrolling is the descent. At each fork station the unwalked siblings appear as stubs that curve off and fold — labeled, dimmed, and tappable (tap a stub to reroute the walk from that fork). Between stations, the fork’s instrument and copy sit as full-width cards threaded on the line. A small persistent glyph keeps the whole-tree shape with a you-dot; tapping it opens the full map (option 11’s sheet). The scrolly track and the tree stop being two things.

Where the fork logic lives
Full-width cards threaded on the line, exactly at the fork they explain — the instrument arrives at the moment its question is asked, at the column’s full size.
How the map survives
Structurally at forks (the stubs are the other branches, in place, tappable) and globally in the corner glyph / pull-up map.
Reuses
Beats map one-to-one onto stations; diagnose() / convertPath drive rerouting; the rail copy becomes the between-station cards nearly verbatim.
Sharpest risk
It’s the biggest build — a third telling, not a fit mode, and it needs a width gate (the wide stage flows stay as they are). Folded stubs must read as “doors you didn’t take,” never “options that vanished.”

Where to probe first

  1. 09, the stepper, first. It is nearly free — BranchMinimap and the beat gates already exist — and it answers the scariest design question on this page in a day: does the section survive the tree leaving center stage? Every other job-split option (10, 11, 12) inherits that answer.
  2. 06 or 10 as small FIT_MODES entries. Both fix the real, current problem — the ~1310px laptop squeeze — without touching the phone story, and both slot into the existing switch beside fit/focus/detail, so they A/B against the shipped three with the control the section already has.
  3. The phone bet is 07 vs 12. One geometry at every width (sideways, degrading to an outline) against a second telling under a width gate (the spine). The section already treats “a different telling” as a legitimate axis — short vs. full on the flow switch — which is an argument that 12 is a third flow, not a fourth fit mode.

These compose rather than compete: 04–06 and 10 are FIT_MODES entries, 07–08 are shape-level, 09 and 11 are chrome, 12 is a flow. One from each column can ship together — e.g. the stepper strip everywhere, the loupe on tight laptops, and the spine (or sheet) below a phone-width gate.
